## CHAPTER 1: GENERAL ACADEMIC POLICIES AND ADMISSIONS

### Section 1.1: Scope and Applicability
These Academic Regulations govern all undergraduate and postgraduate academic programs, enrollment procedures, examination policies, fee structures, and student conduct codes at Apex Institute of Higher Education & Research (hereinafter referred to as "the University"). All matriculated students, faculty members, and academic administrators are subject to the policies set forth herein. 

The University reserves the right to modify, amend, or update these regulations at any time through the Academic Council. Students are expected to familiarize themselves with these regulations upon enrollment. Ignorance of any regulation contained within this document does not constitute a valid defense against academic penalties or administrative sanctions.

### Section 1.2: Academic Calendar and Semester Structure
The academic calendar of the University operates on a semester-based system comprising two primary semesters (Fall Semester and Spring Semester) and an optional Summer Term. 
1. **Fall Semester**: Spans 16 calendar weeks, including 14 weeks of instructional contact, 1 week of mid-semester examinations, and 1 week of final examinations.
2. **Spring Semester**: Spans 16 calendar weeks, featuring an identical breakdown of instructional, mid-semester, and final examination periods.
3. **Summer Term**: An accelerated 8-week term offered at the discretion of the Academic Council primarily for remedial coursework, credit acceleration, or specialized intensive workshops.

Each standard course carries a specific credit hour value, where one credit hour represents 15 hours of formal lecture instruction or 30 hours of supervised laboratory work per semester.

### Section 1.3: Classification of Students
Students registered at the University are categorized based on their total completed credit hours toward their degree program:
- **Freshman / First-Year**: 0 to 29 earned credit hours.
- **Sophomore / Second-Year**: 30 to 59 earned credit hours.
- **Junior / Third-Year**: 60 to 89 earned credit hours.
- **Senior / Fourth-Year**: 90 or more earned credit hours.

Non-degree seeking students, auditing students, and visiting international exchange scholars are classified separately under Special Academic Status and are governed by specific exchange agreements.

## CHAPTER 2: REGISTRATION, CREDIT LOAD AND COURSE ENROLLMENT

### Section 2.1: Credit Load Limits
To maintain full-time student status, an undergraduate student must enroll in a minimum of 12 credit hours per primary semester. The maximum permitted credit load for a full-time undergraduate student in a standard semester is 18 credit hours.

Under exceptional circumstances, a student with a Cumulative Grade Point Average (CGPA) of 3.60 or higher may petition the Department Chair and Academic Dean for a credit overload up to a maximum of 21 credit hours. No student under any circumstances may register for more than 21 credit hours in a single primary semester.

Part-time students are defined as those registered for fewer than 12 credit hours in a primary semester. Part-time status requires prior written approval from the Academic Dean and may impact institutional financial aid eligibility.

### Section 2.2: Add/Drop Period
During the first two weeks of a primary semester (defined as Days 1 through 14 of the published academic calendar), students may add or drop courses online via the Student Information Portal without academic penalty or permanent transcript notation.

Courses dropped during the official Add/Drop period will not appear on the student’s permanent academic transcript, and 100% of the tuition fees associated with the dropped course credit hours will be credited back to the student's financial account.

### Section 2.3: Course Prerequisites and Co-requisites
Students are responsible for satisfying all stated course prerequisites prior to enrolling in advanced courses. Registration software automatically blocks enrollment if prerequisite conditions are unmet. 

In rare cases where a student possesses equivalent prior background knowledge, a Prerequisite Override Form must be signed by the Course Instructor and Department Head and submitted to the Registrar's Office prior to the close of the Add/Drop period.

## CHAPTER 3: ATTENDANCE POLICIES AND EXAM ELIGIBILITY

### Section 3.1: Minimum Attendance Threshold
Regular and punctual attendance at all scheduled lectures, recitations, and laboratory sessions is mandatory for all enrolled students. 

**Clause 3.1.a**: A student must maintain a minimum of 75% attendance in each registered course across the semester to be eligible to sit for the end-semester final examination in that course.

**Clause 3.1.b**: Course instructors track daily attendance electronically. If a student's cumulative attendance drops below 75% at any point prior to final examination week, the instructor issues an automated Attendance Warning notice.

**Clause 3.1.c**: Students whose final attendance percentage at the end of instruction is strictly less than 75% are automatically assigned a grade of "FA" (Failed due to Attendance) and are strictly barred from writing the final examination.

### Section 3.2: Shortage of Attendance Calculation
Attendance is calculated as the ratio of total classes attended by the student to the total number of classes actually conducted by the instructor during the semester, multiplied by 100. 

Official university holidays and authorized institutional deputations (such as representing the University in national athletic competitions or academic symposiums) are counted as attended classes, provided official documentation is submitted to the Dean of Student Affairs within 48 hours of the event.

## CHAPTER 4: TUITION FEES, SCHEDULES AND FINANCIAL POLICIES

### Section 4.1: Tuition Fee Payment Structure
Tuition fees and mandatory institutional charges are billed on a semester basis. All tuition fee invoices are made available through the Student Financial Portal at least 30 days prior to the start of the semester.

### Section 4.2: Payment Due Dates and Grace Period Policy
Full payment of tuition fees, or the first installment under an approved University Deferred Installment Plan, is due no later than 5:00 PM on the official Fee Due Date (typically the Friday preceding the first day of semester classes).

**Clause 4.2.a (Grace Period)**: Students paying tuition fees after the official due date are granted a 7-day grace period (calendar days 1 through 7 post-due date) during which no late payment penalty fee shall be levied or accrued on the account.

**Clause 4.2.b**: Payments received within this 7-day grace period are processed as on-time payments without financial surcharges or administrative holds.

### Section 4.3: Financial Holds and De-registration
If tuition fees remain unpaid after the expiration of the 7-day grace period, a Financial Hold is placed on the student’s record. A Financial Hold prevents the student from viewing semester grades, requesting official transcripts, dropping or adding courses, and registering for subsequent semester terms.

If payment is not received within 21 calendar days following the due date, the Registrar's Office reserves the right to administratively de-register the student from all current courses.

## CHAPTER 5: COURSE WITHDRAWAL AND ADD/DROP PROCEDURES

### Section 5.1: Voluntary Course Withdrawal
After the official two-week Add/Drop period has elapsed, a student may voluntarily withdraw from a course by submitting an electronic Course Withdrawal Form through the Registrar Portal.

### Section 5.2: Maximum Permitted Course Withdrawals
An undergraduate student is permitted a maximum of 4 voluntary course withdrawals throughout their entire degree program duration. Postgraduate students are limited to a maximum of 2 course withdrawals. 

Once a student reaches their lifetime withdrawal allowance, subsequent requests to drop a course after the Add/Drop deadline will be denied, and the student must complete the course and receive a letter grade based on earned evaluation marks.

### Section 5.3: Course Withdrawal Timeline and Transcript Record
**Clause 5.3.a**: A student may formally withdraw from any registered course up to the end of Week 8 of the semester without incurring any academic penalty, and the withdrawn course shall be completely erased from the student's official transcript record.

**Clause 5.3.b**: Course withdrawals submitted prior to the end of Week 8 carry no grade point implications and do not factor into GPA calculations.

## CHAPTER 6: GRADING SYSTEM, CGPA CALCULATION AND ACADEMIC STANDING

### Section 6.1: Letter Grade Scale and Quality Points
Academic performance in all credit-bearing courses is evaluated using a 4.00 grading scale. The letter grades, percentage equivalencies, and quality point values assigned per credit hour are structured as follows:

- **A (Outstanding)**: 90% – 100% | 4.00 Grade Points
- **A- (Excellent)**: 85% – 89% | 3.70 Grade Points
- **B+ (Very Good)**: 80% – 84% | 3.30 Grade Points
- **B (Good)**: 75% – 79% | 3.00 Grade Points
- **B- (Above Average)**: 70% – 74% | 2.70 Grade Points
- **C+ (Average)**: 65% – 69% | 2.30 Grade Points
- **C (Satisfactory Pass)**: 60% – 64% | 2.00 Grade Points
- **C- (Minimum Passing)**: 55% – 59% | 1.70 Grade Points
- **D+ (Marginal)**: 52% – 54% | 1.30 Grade Points
- **D (Pass)**: 50% – 51% | 1.00 Grade Points
- **F (Fail)**: Below 50% | 0.00 Grade Points
- **FA (Failed due to Attendance)**: Unmet attendance | 0.00 Grade Points
- **I (Incomplete)**: Temporary designation | Not computed in GPA
- **W (Withdrawn)**: Formal withdrawal | Not computed in GPA

### Section 6.2: Incomplete Grade Policy
An Incomplete grade ("I") may be granted only when a student has successfully completed at least 75% of the required course coursework with a passing grade but is prevented from completing final term projects or final examinations due to documented emergency or severe illness.

An "I" grade must be resolved and converted to a letter grade by the end of Week 4 of the following primary semester. If the incomplete work is not submitted by this deadline, the "I" grade automatically reverts to an "F".

### Section 6.3: Cumulative Grade Point Average (CGPA) Calculation
The CGPA is calculated by dividing the total cumulative grade quality points earned by the total cumulative credit hours attempted across all semesters at the University. 

Quality points earned for a course equal the credit hour value of the course multiplied by the quality point equivalent of the letter grade received. Passed courses transferred from external institutions count toward total earned credit hours but are excluded from the CGPA calculation.

## CHAPTER 7: ACADEMIC PROBATION, DISMISSAL AND REINSTATEMENT

### Section 7.1: Academic Warning
Any undergraduate student whose Semester Grade Point Average (SGPA) falls below 2.00 in any single semester, despite maintaining a cumulative CGPA above 2.00, will be issued an official Academic Warning by the Registrar. 

A student on Academic Warning is required to meet with an assigned Academic Advisor to formulate a remedial learning plan and is restricted to a maximum course registration load of 14 credit hours in the subsequent term.

### Section 7.2: Academic Probation Thresholds
An undergraduate student is placed on official Academic Probation if their Cumulative Grade Point Average (CGPA) falls below 2.00 at the conclusion of any primary semester.

1. **First Semester on Probation**: The student is limited to a maximum load of 12 credit hours and must participate in mandatory weekly academic tutoring sessions at the Student Learning Center.
2. **Second Consecutive Semester on Probation**: If the student fails to raise their cumulative CGPA to 2.00 or higher after one semester on probation, they are placed on Continued Academic Probation and restricted to 10 credit hours.

### Section 7.3: Academic Dismissal
An undergraduate student who remains on Academic Probation for three consecutive primary semesters without raising their cumulative CGPA to 2.00 is subject to Academic Dismissal from the University.

An administratively dismissed student is ineligible to re-enroll in any degree program at the University for a mandatory cooling-off period of one full academic year (two primary semesters).

### Section 7.4: Reinstatement Appeals Process
Following the mandatory one-year cooling-off period, a dismissed student may petition the Academic Reinstatement Committee for readmission. 

The petition must include:
1. A formal letter explaining the underlying causes of previous academic performance.
2. Official transcripts of academic coursework or professional certifications completed during the dismissal period.
3. A detailed academic improvement plan endorsed by a department faculty sponsor.

Reinstatement is not guaranteed and is granted on a strict conditional basis for one semester.

## CHAPTER 8: MEDICAL ABSENCE, LEAVE OF ABSENCE AND DEFERRALS

### Section 8.1: Documented Medical Absences
Students who miss scheduled classes, mid-term evaluations, or laboratory assignments due to acute illness or emergency hospitalization must report their medical absence to the University Health Center within 48 hours of occurrence.

To be recognized as a valid medical excuse, the student must present a signed medical certificate from a licensed physician registered with the State Medical Board, complete with hospital registration stamps and diagnostic dates.

### Section 8.2: Make-Up Examination Eligibility
If a student misses a scheduled mid-semester examination due to an approved medical absence or bereavement in the immediate family (parents, siblings, spouse, children), the course instructor must administer a make-up examination within 10 business days of the student's return to campus.

Make-up examinations must be equal in rigor and coverage to the original examination. No make-up examination fee may be charged to students with verified medical excuses.

### Section 8.3: Voluntary Leave of Absence
A student in good academic standing (CGPA 2.00 or higher) may apply for a formal Voluntary Leave of Absence for personal, financial, or medical reasons for up to two consecutive primary semesters.

During an approved Leave of Absence, the student retains their matriculated status and catalog rights. The duration of an approved leave does not count toward the maximum allowable degree completion timeframe.

## CHAPTER 9: ACADEMIC INTEGRITY, PLAGIARISM AND DISCIPLINARY CODES

### Section 9.1: Definition of Academic Misconduct
Apex Institute of Higher Education & Research strictly adheres to the highest standards of academic honesty. Academic misconduct includes, but is not limited to:
- **Plagiarism**: Incorporating the published or unpublished work, code, data, or ideas of another person without full and proper attribution.
- **Cheating**: Utilizing unauthorized materials, devices, notes, or peer communication during quizzes, tests, or final examinations.
- **Fabrication**: Falsifying laboratory data, research observations, clinical records, or academic citations.
- **Collusion**: Assisting another student in performing unauthorized collective work on individual assignments.

### Section 9.2: Disciplinary Sanctions for Integrity Violations
Violations of the Academic Integrity Code are categorized into minor and major offenses:

1. **First-Time Minor Violation** (e.g., inadequate citation in a term paper): Handled directly by the Course Instructor. Sanctions range from a mandatory paper resubmission with a grade cap to a score of zero on the specific assignment.
2. **Major Violation** (e.g., cheating on a final exam, buying term papers): Referred immediately to the University Judicial Board. Sanctions include automatic course failure ("F" grade), suspension for 1 to 2 semesters, or permanent expulsion from the University.

All disciplinary decisions rendered by the University Judicial Board are recorded in the student’s confidential disciplinary file maintained by the Dean of Students.

## CHAPTER 10: DEGREE COMPLETION, HONORS ELIGIBILITY AND GRADUATION

### Section 10.1: Graduation Requirements
To qualify for the award of a Bachelor’s Degree, an undergraduate student must satisfy all of the following requirements:
1. Successful completion of a minimum of 120 credit hours as specified in the relevant program curriculum.
2. Achievement of a cumulative CGPA of at least 2.00 across all attempted coursework.
3. Completion of a minimum of 60 credit hours in residence at Apex Institute of Higher Education & Research.
4. Fulfillment of all departmental core, elective, general education, and capstone project requirements.
5. Settlement of all outstanding financial obligations, tuition balances, and library fines.

### Section 10.2: Maximum Time Limit for Degree Completion
Undergraduate students are expected to complete their degree program within 4 academic years (8 primary semesters). The maximum allowable time limit to fulfill degree requirements is 6 academic years (12 primary semesters) from the initial date of matriculation.

Students who exceed the 6-year maximum limit are subject to academic review and may be required to re-take out-of-date core courses or re-apply for program admission under current catalog requirements.

### Section 10.3: Graduation Latin Honors
Graduating undergraduate seniors who have completed at least 60 credit hours in residence and maintained exemplary academic standing are awarded Latin Honors based on cumulative CGPA at graduation:

- **Summa Cum Laude (With Highest Honor)**: CGPA 3.90 – 4.00
- **Magna Cum Laude (With High Honor)**: CGPA 3.75 – 3.89
- **Cum Laude (With Honor)**: CGPA 3.50 – 3.74

Latin Honors designations are printed on the official diploma scroll and permanent academic transcript.

## CHAPTER 12: DORMITORY AND HOSTEL ACCOMMODATION RULES

### Section 12.1: Campus Residence Eligibility and Allocation
On-campus dormitory accommodation is offered to full-time matriculated students registered for a minimum of 12 credit hours per semester. Priority for residential room allocation is given to first-year undergraduate students and international exchange scholars. Room assignments are conducted annually through a randomized lottery system administered by the Housing Office during the month of June.

### Section 12.2: Occupancy Agreements and Quiet Hours
All hostel residents must sign a mandatory Housing License Agreement prior to occupancy. Standard residential quiet hours are enforced in all dormitories from 10:00 PM to 7:00 AM on weeknights (Sunday through Thursday) and from 12:00 AM midnight to 8:00 AM on weekends (Friday and Saturday). Violations of quiet hours result in written warnings, and three recorded infractions lead to housing probation.

### Section 12.3: Damage Deposit and Maintenance Fees
A refundable security deposit of $300 is charged to all residential students upon room check-in. The deposit is refunded upon check-out at the end of the academic term, minus deductions for structural room damage, missing furniture, or unreturned room keys. Room cancellations made prior to July 15 receive a 100% deposit refund, whereas cancellations post July 15 forfeit the entire security deposit.

### Section 13.1: Student Health Center Services
The University Health Center provides routine primary care, emergency first aid, immunizations, and preventive health consultations to registered students. Basic outpatient consultations at the Health Center are covered by the mandatory Student Health Fee paid during semester registration.

### Section 13.2: Counseling and Psychological Services (CAPS)
The CAPS office provides confidential short-term psychological counseling, crisis intervention, and mental health workshops. Students are entitled to up to 8 free individual counseling sessions per academic year. Referrals to specialized off-campus clinical providers are arranged for long-term therapeutic care.

### Section 13.3: Mandatory Immunization Requirements
Prior to initial course registration, all incoming domestic and international students must submit proof of immunization against Measles, Mumps, Rubella (MMR), Hepatitis B, and Meningococcal Meningitis. Students failing to provide verified immunization records to the Student Health Portal within 14 days of matriculation will have an Administrative Hold placed on their registration.

### Section 15.1: Full-Time Enrollment Requirement for F-1 Visa Holders
International students attending the University on F-1 student visas are required by federal regulations to maintain full-time course enrollment (minimum 12 credit hours for undergraduates, 9 credit hours for postgraduates) during all mandatory primary semesters. OPT applications for post-graduation employment must be submitted to the International Student Office up to 90 days before degree completion.

## CHAPTER 16: FINANCIAL AID, SCHOLARSHIPS AND WORK-STUDY EMPLOYMENT

### Section 16.1: Merit-Based Merit Scholarships
The University awards Institutional Merit Scholarships to high-achieving undergraduate students based on entrance examination scores and cumulative CGPA. Merit scholarship recipients must maintain a minimum cumulative CGPA of 3.20 at the conclusion of each academic year to retain scholarship renewal eligibility.

### Section 16.2: Satisfactory Academic Progress (SAP) for Financial Aid
To remain eligible for federal, state, and institutional financial aid grants, students must maintain Satisfactory Academic Progress (SAP). SAP requires maintaining a minimum cumulative CGPA of 2.00 and successfully completing at least 67% of all cumulative attempted credit hours.

### Section 16.3: Federal Work-Study Program
Students awarded Federal Work-Study funding are eligible for part-time campus employment up to a maximum of 20 hours per week during active instructional semesters and up to 40 hours per week during official semester breaks. Work-study earnings are disbursed bi-weekly directly to the student's personal bank account.

## CHAPTER 22: LABORATORY SAFETY PROTOCOLS AND HAZARDOUS MATERIALS

### Section 22.1: Mandatory Safety Orientation and Personal Protective Equipment (PPE)
All students enrolled in courses involving physical, chemical, biological, or engineering laboratories must complete the online Environmental Health & Safety (EHS) Safety Orientation prior to their first lab session. 

**Clause 22.1.a**: Appropriate Personal Protective Equipment (PPE)—including ANSI-certified safety goggles, closed-toe footwear, flame-resistant lab coats, and nitrile gloves—must be worn at all times in active laboratory environments. Students arriving without compliant PPE will be denied entry to the laboratory session and marked as unexcused absent.

**Clause 22.1.b**: Eating, drinking, storing food, applying cosmetics, or handling personal mobile electronic devices is strictly forbidden inside all scientific research and instructional laboratories.

### Section 22.2: Chemical and Hazardous Waste Disposal
Hazardous chemical wastes, biological waste items, sharps, and contaminated materials must be segregated at the point of generation and disposed of in designated labeled containers in accordance with state environmental protection guidelines. 

Under no circumstances may hazardous chemicals, organic solvents, heavy metal solutions, or biohazardous agents be poured down sink drains or disposed of in standard trash receptacles. Violations of hazardous waste protocols will result in immediate laboratory suspension and referral to the University Safety Committee.

## CHAPTER 23: STUDENT ADVISORY COUNCILS AND DEPARTMENTAL REPRESENTATION

### Section 23.1: Student Representation on Academic Committees
The University recognizes the importance of student input in academic governance. Each academic department maintains a Departmental Student Advisory Council (DSAC) comprising elected student representatives from each class year.

Student representatives on DSAC meet monthly with the Department Chair and faculty curriculum committee to provide feedback on course offerings, teaching effectiveness, laboratory infrastructure, and academic advising support.

During the final two weeks of instruction in every primary semester, students are required to complete an anonymous Student Evaluation of Teaching (SET) survey for each course in which they are enrolled. 

SET survey results are compiled confidentially by the Office of Institutional Effectiveness and released to faculty members and Department Chairs after final semester grades have been officially submitted to the Registrar. SET data is utilized in annual faculty performance reviews and tenure decisions.

## CHAPTER 24: DISTANCE LEARNING, ONLINE COURSEWORK AND PROCTORING

### Section 24.1: Classification of Distance Learning Courses
The University offers courses through three primary instructional delivery modalities:
1. **Face-to-Face (F2F)**: Traditional on-campus classroom instruction where 100% of contact hours occur in physical lecture halls.
2. **Hybrid / Blended**: Coursework combining on-campus physical lectures (50% to 70%) with asynchronous online learning modules (30% to 50%).
3. **Fully Online (Asynchronous or Synchronous)**: 100% of instruction and course materials are delivered through the university Learning Management System (LMS).

### Section 24.2: Automated Remote Exam Proctoring Guidelines
For fully online courses, mid-term and final examinations may be administered through authorized remote automated proctoring software. 

**Clause 24.2.a**: Students taking remotely proctored examinations must possess a functional webcam, microphone, stable high-speed internet connection, and a private quiet room free from third-party individuals or unauthorized reference materials.

**Clause 24.2.b**: The proctoring system flags unusual eye movements, background audio, or secondary software applications. Flagged examination sessions are reviewed manually by the course instructor and the Academic Integrity Board within 5 business days.

## CHAPTER 25: CAMPUS EMERGENCY PROTOCOLS, EVACUATION AND SAFETY

### Section 25.1: Emergency Notification System (Apex Alert)
The University operates the "Apex Alert" emergency notification system, which dispatches immediate text messages, automated voice calls, email notifications, and desktop alerts during active campus emergencies, severe weather closures, or security lockdowns.

All enrolled students are automatically subscribed to Apex Alert upon registration using their registered emergency contact numbers. Students are required to review and update their emergency contact information annually in the Student Information Portal.

### Section 25.2: Evacuation Drills and Building Security
Fire and emergency evacuation drills are conducted unannounced in all academic buildings, research facilities, and residential dormitories once per semester by Campus Safety officers. 

Upon hearing an audible building alarm or siren, all occupants must immediately evacuate the building using the nearest designated emergency exit stairs and assemble at designated assembly areas at least 150 feet away from the structure. Elevators must not be used during fire evacuations.

## CHAPTER 26: DEFERRALS, DEFERRED PAYMENT AGREEMENTS AND BURSAR REFUNDS

### Section 26.1: Deferred Payment Agreement (DPA) Terms
Students unable to pay full semester tuition by the published fee due date may apply for a Deferred Payment Agreement (DPA) through the Bursar’s Office prior to the end of the registration period.

Under a DPA, total semester tuition and fees are divided into three equal monthly installments:
- **First Installment (34%)**: Due on the official Fee Due Date prior to Week 1.
- **Second Installment (33%)**: Due on the Friday of Week 5 of the semester.
- **Third Installment (33%)**: Due on the Friday of Week 9 of the semester.

A non-refundable administration fee of $35 is assessed upon DPA approval. Failure to pay any DPA installment by its specified due date incurs a $25 installment late fee and places a temporary financial registration hold on the account.

### Section 26.2: Direct Deposit Electronic Refund Processing
Tuition refunds resulting from dropped courses, overpayments, or disbursed financial aid grants are processed electronically via Direct Deposit to the student's designated personal checking or savings account.

Students must set up Direct Deposit banking details in the Financial Portal. Electronic refund transfers are processed weekly on Tuesdays and Thursdays. Paper refund checks are issued only for students without valid direct deposit information and require 10 to 14 business days for postal delivery.

## CHAPTER 27: STUDENT RECORDS PRIVACY AND FERPA COMPLIANCE

### Section 27.1: Protection of Educational Records
In accordance with the Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act (FERPA) and institutional privacy standards, the University maintains the confidentiality of all student educational records, grade transcripts, financial accounts, and disciplinary files.

No educational records or academic performance indicators will be disclosed to third parties—including parents, guardians, spouses, or external prospective employers—without the express written consent of the student, except as authorized by law or court subpoena.

### Section 27.2: Directory Information Waivers
The University designates certain basic items as public "Directory Information," including: student name, local address, major field of study, enrollment status (full-time/part-time), dates of attendance, degrees and honors conferred, and participation in officially recognized athletic teams.

Students who wish to suppress the release of Directory Information must submit a formal FERPA Non-Disclosure Request Form to the Office of the Registrar within the first 10 business days of the Fall semester.

## CHAPTER 28: RESEARCH ETHICS, HUMAN SUBJECTS AND INSTITUTIONAL REVIEW BOARD

### Section 28.1: Institutional Review Board (IRB) Approval Requirements
All research projects involving human subjects—including surveys, interviews, behavioral observations, clinical trials, or analysis of identifiable personal data conducted by undergraduate or graduate students—must receive prior review and approval from the Institutional Review Board (IRB).

Research activities involving human subjects initiated without prior formal written IRB approval are in violation of institutional research ethics codes, and data collected under such unauthorized studies cannot be published, presented, or submitted for academic credit.

### Section 28.2: Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee (IACUC)
Research involving vertebrate animals conducted in university laboratories or field stations must be submitted to and approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee (IACUC). 

All student researchers working with laboratory animals must complete mandatory humane care, handling, and safety training modules provided by the Office of Research Compliance prior to commencing animal research protocols.

## CHAPTER 29: ALUMNI SERVICES, TRANSITION SERVICES AND DEGREE AUDITS

### Section 29.1: Alumni Association Membership
Upon official conferral of a Bachelor's or Master's degree by the Board of Trustees, all graduates automatically become life members of the Apex University Alumni Association. Life membership grants access to regional alumni chapters, career networking platforms, digital library database access, and invitations to annual homecoming reunions.

### Section 29.2: Automated Degree Audit System (DegreeWorks)
Students may track their progress toward degree completion at any time using the automated Degree Audit System. The degree audit report evaluates completed coursework, current registrations, transfer credits, and remaining core requirements against the matriculation catalog year. Students are advised to perform a degree audit at the beginning of each class registration cycle.

## CHAPTER 30: FACULTY ADVISING, UNDERGRADUATE MENTORSHIP AND CAMPUS TUTORING

### Section 30.1: Faculty Advising Assignment
Upon matriculation, every undergraduate student is assigned a dedicated Faculty Academic Advisor within their declared academic major department. Students who have not yet declared a major are assigned an Academic Advising Center Counselor.

Students are required to meet with their assigned Faculty Advisor at least once per primary semester prior to the opening of course registration window. Advising holds prevent registration until the student's planned course schedule is reviewed and approved by the advisor.

### Section 30.2: Peer Tutoring and Learning Center Support
The University Learning Center provides free peer tutoring services in core subjects, including introductory mathematics, general chemistry, physics, computer programming, and writing composition. Tutoring is available on a walk-in basis or by appointment via the Student Success Portal.

Students experiencing academic difficulty in specific courses are encouraged to utilize Learning Center services early in the semester. Faculty members may formally refer struggling students to the Learning Center through early alert academic notices.
